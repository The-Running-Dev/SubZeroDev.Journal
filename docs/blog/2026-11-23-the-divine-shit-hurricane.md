# The Divine Shit Hurricane

Lucifer was having a perfectly pleasant afternoon in Hell when an incident report arrived from Earth.

He read it once.

Then again.

Then he stared at the ceiling.

> **Lucifer:** "Father?"
>
> **God:** "Yes, Lucifer?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Remember that particular human we discussed? The one who keeps getting into situations so fucking ridiculous that even I struggle to believe they're real?"
>
> **God:** "Which one?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "The American living in Bulgaria."
>
> **God:** "Oh, for fuck's sake. What now?"

<!-- truncate -->

## Act I: The Property Investment from Hell

Lucifer materialized a chair, poured himself a drink, and began.

> **Lucifer:** "Well, Father, apparently he thought helping his family resolve a property dispute was a good idea."
>
> **God:** "That sounds noble."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Yes. That's what makes it so fucking funny."

You see, the human had returned to Bulgaria, where his family owned a house.

A perfectly ordinary house.

With a yard.

And a driveway.

And several family members who apparently believed that *The Hunger Games* was an instructional documentary on inheritance law.

> **God:** "Surely they could have talked things through?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Father, these people could turn a discussion about the weather into a fucking blood feud."
>
> **God:** "Did he at least get to enjoy the house?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Well, he continued paying rent somewhere else because he couldn't trust what would happen."
>
> **God:** "So he had two places to live?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "More accurately, he had two places to worry about."
>
> **God:** "And what did he gain?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Legal expenses, lost peace, and a new appreciation for the phrase 'spectacular failure of strategic vision.'"

God leaned back.

> **God:** "Perhaps he could have handled it better."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Oh, absolutely. He admits that himself. That's actually one of the few fucking miracles in this story."

## Act II: Enter the Shit Hurricane

Unfortunately, the family dispute involved Elizabeth.

Elizabeth was a woman whose relentless personality her son had once admired.

He had mistaken it for strength.

Determination.

Conviction.

The kind of person who would stand her ground when the world turned against her.

Over the years, however, he had begun to suspect that what he had admired as extraordinary strength might sometimes be an extraordinary inability to stop fighting.

> **Lucifer:** "You see, Father, there's a difference between strength of character and the emotional regulation of an angry rhinoceros."
>
> **God:** "Rhinoceroses are generally peaceful animals."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Then I apologize to the rhinoceroses."

As the dispute escalated, Elizabeth's involvement became, in her son's estimation, the meteorological equivalent of a Category 5 shit hurricane.

Not a gentle summer breeze of family disagreement.

Not a temporary storm of misunderstandings.

A full-scale Jim Lahey-certified shit hurricane, complete with flying lawyers, shattered relationships, and legal paperwork circulating in the upper atmosphere.

> **God:** "Could anyone stop it?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Father, you parted the Red Sea. I wouldn't put money on you parting this woman's anger."

And the extraordinary thing?

The human had eventually reached a conclusion.

He didn't want revenge.

He didn't want to become more vicious.

He didn't want to demonstrate just how much damage he could inflict if he truly abandoned restraint.

He simply wanted the fucking hurricane to go somewhere else.

## Act III: The Car of Brother Mario

And now, dear reader, we arrive at the part where even the Devil begins questioning the competence of the universe's screenwriters.

There was a car.

Not just any car.

The car belonged to Brother Mario.

Mario was the human's beloved uncle, a man who had been far more than an uncle. A brother in every meaningful sense.

Mario had died.

His car, however, had apparently been assigned a new career.

**Driveway obstruction.**

The human's cousin had been using Mario's car to block access to the property.

And one day, the human moved it.

That's it.

He moved the fucking car.

> **God:** "And?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Criminal proceedings."
>
> **God:** "For stealing the car?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "No."
>
> **God:** "For destroying the car?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "No."
>
> **God:** "Then what?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "For moving the fucking car, Father."
>
> **God:** "..."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Exactly."

A family property dispute had somehow evolved into legal proceedings involving the relocation of a deceased relative's automobile.

Somewhere, a lawyer was preparing arguments.

Somewhere, a court had paperwork.

Somewhere, public resources were being consumed.

And somewhere in heaven, Brother Mario was about to discover the funniest thing that had happened since his departure from Earth.

## Act IV: Meanwhile, in Heaven

Mario was enjoying a peaceful afternoon when a celestial notification appeared.

**SUBJECT: YOUR AUTOMOBILE**

He opened it.

Read the report.

And began laughing.

Not chuckling.

Not smiling politely.

Absolutely fucking howling.

> **Mario:** "YOU'RE FUCKING KIDDING ME!"
>
> **Saint Peter:** "Is something wrong?"
>
> **Mario:** "Wrong? My nephew is in legal trouble because someone blocked a driveway with MY FUCKING CAR!"
>
> **Saint Peter:** "I'm terribly sorry."
>
> **Mario:** "SORRY? THIS IS THE FUNNIEST FUCKING THING I'VE EVER HEARD!"

Mario laughed so hard that several angels had to stop rehearsing.

He looked down at Earth.

There was his nephew.

The same stubborn bastard who had spent years surviving adversity, searching for peace, reading the Stoics, studying Zen, contemplating the Tao, and trying to understand the nature of existence.

And now?

He was contemplating the legal consequences of moving a fucking car.

Mario wiped a tear from his eye.

> **Mario:** "Oh, brother. You'll get used to it."

And began laughing again.

## Act V: Wu Wei and the Fucking Court Summons

Back in Hell, Lucifer was still examining the incident report.

> **Lucifer:** "And here's the truly magnificent part, Father. The human has spent years studying the philosophy of non-forcing."
>
> **God:** "Wu wei?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Precisely."
>
> **God:** "Then surely he understands that one should not force circumstances."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Yes. Except circumstances keep forcing themselves upon HIM."

The human had observed something peculiar about his life.

He rarely went looking for extraordinary experiences.

They simply found him.

Sometimes those experiences were beautiful.

Moments of peace.

Unexpected friendships.

Insights into existence that left him quietly smiling at the universe.

Other times?

A fucking criminal proceeding over Brother Mario's car.

> **Lucifer:** "Perhaps the Tao doesn't discriminate between enlightenment and absolute fucking nonsense."
>
> **God:** "The Tao simply flows."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Yes, Father. And occasionally it flows directly into a Bulgarian courtroom."

Alan Watts would have needed another drink.

Marcus Aurelius would have opened his journal.

Epictetus would have reminded everyone that some things are outside our control.

And Jim Lahey would have looked at the approaching weather system and immediately recognized the atmospheric conditions.

**Shit winds, Randy. Shit winds.**

## Act VI: Hindsight Is 20/20

Eventually, the human sat down and examined the entire disaster.

And arrived at a conclusion.

> "I should never have gotten involved in this fucking fight."

Not because he was afraid.

Not because he couldn't fight.

But because there had never been enough to gain to justify everything he had sacrificed.

Time.

Money.

Energy.

Peace.

He had entered the conflict imagining that something worthwhile might come from it.

Instead, he'd acquired a collection of legal problems and a graduate-level education in the economics of family dysfunction.

He could have handled certain things better.

He should perhaps have called the authorities earlier.

He could have made different decisions.

Should have.

Could have.

Would have.

But none of those words came with a time machine.

> **God:** "So what will he do?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Deal with the consequences."
>
> **God:** "Will he survive?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Father, this man has survived far worse. That's not even in question."
>
> **God:** "Is he angry?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Oh, he's fucking furious."
>
> **God:** "And what does he intend to do with that anger?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Apparently, nothing particularly dramatic. Which must be enormously disappointing to the rest of the family."

And then the human said something remarkable about Elizabeth.

After describing the anger, the damage, the vindictiveness he believed had driven her behavior, and the enormous cost of the entire conflict, he concluded:

**"May she find her peace."**

Lucifer stopped reading.

> **Lucifer:** "Well, fuck me."
>
> **God:** "What?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "After all that, he wishes her peace."
>
> **God:** "Perhaps he's finally learning."
>
> **Lucifer:** "Or perhaps he's simply exhausted every other available option."

God smiled.

> **God:** "Does it matter?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Not particularly."

## Epilogue: The Last Laugh

Somewhere on Earth, a man was preparing to face the consequences of decisions he could no longer change.

A house remained disputed.

Legal proceedings continued.

Money would be spent.

The past would remain stubbornly, offensively uneditable.

And the man who had spent so long pursuing peace had discovered that sometimes the greatest strategic achievement was recognizing that a battle should never have been fought.

He looked at the whole ridiculous affair.

Shook his head.

And said:

**"Oh well."**

Not surrender.

Not defeat.

Just two words of acceptance, delivered with the weary amusement of a man who had seen enough of life's bullshit to recognize another installment.

Up in heaven, Brother Mario was still laughing.

Down in Hell, Lucifer raised his glass.

> **Lucifer:** "To Brother Mario."
>
> **God:** "To Brother Mario."
>
> **Lucifer:** "And to the most expensive fucking driveway incident in the history of this family's emotional dysfunction."

They drank.

For a moment, the universe was quiet.

Then God leaned forward.

> **God:** "Lucifer?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Yes, Father?"
>
> **God:** "Do you think he'll finally have some peace?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "I certainly fucking hope so."
>
> **God:** "And if he doesn't?"
>
> **Lucifer:** "Then we'll be back here next week, won't we?"

From somewhere above came the unmistakable sound of Brother Mario laughing his ass off.

Lucifer looked toward heaven.

Smiled.

And raised his glass once more.

**"Well, at least someone's getting their money's worth out of this fucking circus."**
