# I Stopped Asking the Universe Questions

*On Zen, brief moments of clarity, the possibility of a monastery, and a kind of peace I no longer need to explain.*

I've been considering something.

Not a decision.

Not a plan.

Just an idea that has stayed with me.

What if I went to a Zen monastery somewhere?

For a few years.

Not a weekend retreat. Not a spiritual holiday. Not a photograph of myself looking contemplative beside a mountain.

Actually living there.

Stepping away from my ordinary life and spending years immersed in something entirely different.

I don't know whether I'll ever do it.

But I keep returning to the possibility.

And when I was asked why, I realized the answer was remarkably simple.

**Peace.**

<!-- truncate -->

## The Questions

When we're younger, we tend to have questions.

Why are we here?

What's the meaning of life?

What's the meaning of the universe?

Why does any of this exist?

Why this? Why that?

I remember the importance of questions like these.

There's something compelling about the idea that somewhere, if you search hard enough, there must be an answer that explains everything.

I've spent much of my life questioning things.

People. Systems. Ideas. Beliefs.

I still ask questions about things that interest me.

I still love learning.

But something has changed.

I no longer find myself asking those enormous questions about the meaning of existence.

Not because I've solved them.

I haven't.

Not because I've discovered some grand metaphysical truth that everyone else has overlooked.

I haven't done that either.

I simply don't feel the need anymore.

**It just is what it is.**

And that realization is surprisingly peaceful.

## The Little Smile

In my exploration of Zen and Buddhism, I've encountered descriptions of moments of clarity.

A quiet instant.

A sudden understanding that isn't necessarily expressed through words.

A small smile.

And then the moment passes.

You return to ordinary life.

You forget.

Or perhaps you simply lose contact with whatever seemed so clear for that little while.

I've had moments that remind me of those descriptions.

Not frequently.

Not continuously.

And I'm certainly not claiming enlightenment.

I wouldn't even claim to understand exactly what happened.

But I've experienced brief moments when something I've been learning about seems less like an idea and more like something immediately recognizable.

No elaborate argument.

No need to explain it to somebody.

Just a quiet sense of understanding.

For a little while.

Then life continues.

And the clarity fades.

I don't find that invalidates the experience.

A moment doesn't have to last forever to have happened.

## Lucifer Has Nothing to Sell

Usually, I imagine Lucifer having something sarcastic to say about absolutely everything.

Humanity provides him with an inexhaustible supply of material.

But this feels different.

In my imagination, he listens as I describe those brief moments.

"So, brother, you've found the meaning of life?"

"No."

"Discovered the secret of the universe?"

"No."

"Achieved enlightenment?"

"Absolutely not."

"Then what have you discovered?"

I think about the question.

"That I don't need to know."

And for once, he doesn't laugh.

## Studying Without Becoming

I've spent years learning from Stoicism.

More recently, Zen and Buddhism have become important to me.

I notice connections between the traditions.

I practice some of what they teach.

Sometimes I understand an idea intellectually.

Occasionally I experience something that gives that idea a different kind of meaning.

But I don't need to declare myself a Stoic, a Buddhist, or a Zen practitioner of some particular rank.

I don't need a spiritual identity to validate an experience.

And I don't need to pretend that a few quiet moments mean I've understood everything.

There's still plenty I don't know.

Probably always will be.

That's fine.

## The Monastery

Which brings me back to that monastery.

I wonder what it would be like to spend years in a place where the ordinary demands of my life were replaced by a different rhythm.

Where the practice wasn't something fitted around everything else.

Where the days themselves were shaped by it.

Would it change me?

I suspect it would.

How?

I have no fucking idea.

Perhaps it wouldn't resemble the experience I imagine at all.

I haven't decided to go.

I haven't even decided whether I want to make it a real plan.

It's simply a possibility I find myself considering.

Not because I want to become a monk.

Not because I want to return with a title.

Not because I believe enlightenment is waiting for me at the end of a mountain path.

Peace.

That was the answer.

And it's still the answer.

## Nothing to Resolve

I've spent plenty of time trying to understand things.

I've also learned that not everything in life is going to yield to explanation.

Sometimes an answer matters.

Sometimes a question is worth pursuing for years.

And sometimes the question itself simply stops being important.

I'm not suggesting this is some universal destination everyone eventually reaches.

It's simply where I find myself now.

I still have questions.

Just not those questions.

And occasionally, I experience one of those little moments.

A bit of clarity.

A quiet smile.

Nothing extraordinary needs to happen.

Then it passes.

And I continue living.

Somewhere in the imaginary room, Discount Marcus Aurelius opens his mouth to deliver an ancient lesson.

Lucifer gently gestures for silence.

Not because we've finally discovered the answer.

But because, for once, nobody needs to ask another question.

**It just is what it is.**
