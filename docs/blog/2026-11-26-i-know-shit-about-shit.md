# I Know Shit About Shit

*An unauthorized philosophical declaration by a man who refuses to claim he knows what the fuck he's talking about.*

I have a confession.

**I know shit about shit.**

There.

I've said it.

I am not a philosopher. I am not a psychologist. I am not some enlightened sage floating six inches above the ground while contemplating the cosmic significance of a fucking cucumber.

I'm a guy who likes learning things.

I read. I listen. I think. I ask questions.

And I argue.

Oh, Lord, do I argue.

Give me an interesting subject and I'll happily debate it for hours.

Human psychology? Absolutely.

Philosophy? Why not?

Technology? Naturally.

Whether people can fundamentally change? Fuck, pull up a chair.

And here's something that apparently confuses people.

**I am perfectly capable of arguing a position and being wrong.**

<!-- truncate -->

Happens often enough.

So fucking what?

## The Commander Has Entered the Chat

According to my Myers-Briggs personality results, I'm a Commander.

One hundred percent assertive.

Now, I'm not presenting that personality test as some immutable scientific definition of my soul.

It's a description I recognize in myself.

I'm assertive.

I have opinions.

I'll defend those opinions.

And if someone comes along with a better argument, evidence I've overlooked, or an experience that challenges my assumptions, excellent!

Perhaps I've learned something.

How fucking marvelous.

Lucifer appeared in my imagination, looking offended.

"Brother, are you suggesting that admitting ignorance is acceptable?"

"Yes."

"Even when someone disagrees with you?"

"Especially then."

"Good God. You'll never survive modern discourse."

## The Absolute Truth Department

What drives me up the wall isn't disagreement.

It's the declaration of absolute truth where none has been established.

Someone tells me:

"In my experience, people don't change."

Perfectly reasonable.

That's their experience.

We can talk about it.

Maybe they've known people who repeated the same behaviors for decades.

Maybe they've seen promises broken and old patterns reappear.

I can understand why they might reach that conclusion.

But then comes the next sentence.

"People don't change. That's just how it is."

Ah.

There it is.

We've gone from personal observation to a universal law of the human condition.

Apparently, the entire species has been surveyed and the results are final.

Lucifer leaned back.

"How efficient. Eight billion people, and somebody has already completed the research."

"Exactly."

"Presumably peer-reviewed?"

"By their own fucking experience."

"Excellent. The strongest possible sample size of one."

And that's where the debate begins.

Not because I need everyone to agree with me.

But because a person's experience, however legitimate, doesn't automatically establish a universal truth.

**Your experience is valid. It isn't necessarily everybody else's.**

Neither is mine.

That's the part people sometimes seem to miss.

I'm not asking you to replace your certainty with mine.

I'm questioning whether certainty is warranted at all.

## Being Wrong Is Not a Personal Emergency

I've changed my mind about plenty of things.

I expect I'll change it again.

My understanding today is not guaranteed to be my understanding tomorrow.

Why would it be?

I haven't stopped experiencing life.

I haven't stopped learning.

And I certainly haven't acquired the final fucking edition of myself.

This applies to beliefs, opinions, even identity.

Things can evolve.

Some may remain remarkably stable.

Others may change so substantially that our younger selves would barely recognize the people we've become.

I don't have a universal formula for any of that.

And I'm not particularly interested in pretending I do.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius appeared with a battered notebook.

"A wise man examines his own judgments."

"Exactly, Marcus."

"And welcomes correction."

"Yes!"

"And refrains from claiming knowledge he does not possess."

"NOW WE'RE FUCKING TALKING!"

He looked pleased.

Lucifer looked suspicious.

"Careful, brother. At this rate he'll charge you for a philosophy subscription."

## Debate Is Not a Declaration of War

Here's another thing.

A debate doesn't automatically become personal because the participants disagree.

I can spend hours challenging somebody's ideas and still like the person.

I can respect their intelligence while disagreeing with their conclusions.

I can say, "That's your opinion," without meaning, "You're an idiot."

And I can accept hearing that my own opinion is mistaken.

Of course, I can also be abrasive.

I'm not going to pretend my delivery is always a model of diplomatic restraint.

Sometimes I press hard.

Sometimes I get carried away.

Sometimes the person opposite me probably wishes they'd started a conversation about the fucking weather.

Fair enough.

But there's an enormous difference between defending an argument and believing your entire identity depends on never conceding a point.

I'm not interested in treating disagreement as a personal humiliation.

I'd rather learn something.

## The Final Edition Does Not Exist

Lucifer contemplated this for a moment.

"So, brother, what do you actually know?"

"Some things."

"With certainty?"

"Depends on the thing."

"And everything else?"

"I keep learning."

"Good heavens. What a profoundly inconvenient approach to dogmatism."

I smiled.

Knowledge can be personal in how we acquire it, understand it, and apply it.

That doesn't mean every factual claim becomes equally true because someone believes it.

It means our understanding has limits.

Our perspectives are incomplete.

And when we mistake the boundaries of our own experience for the boundaries of reality, we stop asking useful questions.

Discount Marcus closed his notebook.

"Perhaps wisdom is recognizing what one does not know."

"Perhaps, Marcus."

"And perhaps one ought to speak less."

"Don't push your fucking luck."

Lucifer raised his glass.

"To a man who knows shit about shit."

"And is perfectly happy to admit it."

"May you continue to be wrong occasionally."

"I fully expect to."

**Because being wrong doesn't threaten who I am. Refusing to learn might.**
