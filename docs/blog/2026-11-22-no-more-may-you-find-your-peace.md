# No More — May You Find Your Peace

What a fucking couple of weeks.

The BMW's engine is in shambles. The Mazda is stranded somewhere in northern Bulgaria with busted rear struts. The e-bike ended up with a destroyed rear tire after I lent it to someone. I got drunk, took too many drugs, fell on my face at least twice trying to ride the damn thing, and lost my phone for two days.

The police stopped me. Then, in an entirely separate matter, I discovered that apparently being called in as a witness doesn't necessarily mean they aren't treating you as the person responsible.

Welcome to Absurdia Bulgaria.

And somewhere in the middle of all this, I shaved my head smoother than a baby's bottom and sat down to watch Bruce Willis, Morgan Freeman, and John Malkovich in *RED*.

Retired Extremely Dangerous.

Fucking hilarious movie.

Because apparently, when life becomes an absolute shitshow, the appropriate response is to polish your skull and watch aging assassins blow things up.

<!-- truncate -->

But underneath all the mechanical disasters, bureaucratic nonsense, and absurd comedy, something much more significant has been happening.

**I've been saying goodbye to people I've spent my entire life caring about.**

And I never imagined I would be here.

## Fifty years of showing up

I'm fifty years old.

For most of those years, I believed that loving people meant putting them ahead of myself.

Family. Friends. Marriage. Relationships.

That's what you were supposed to do, wasn't it?

You showed up. You helped. You sacrificed. You gave what you could, sometimes more than you could afford.

And I did.

I helped people financially. I gave them my time, my attention, my home, and my energy. I was the older brother who took care of things. The friend who was there when somebody needed something.

I didn't keep score.

I didn't expect repayment.

**I simply believed that people mattered more than I did.**

And there was my mistake.

Not caring about people. Not being generous. But believing that caring for others required neglecting myself.

I taught people, over decades, that I would always be there. That I would tolerate their behavior. That I would make the effort. That I would maintain the relationship.

And people got used to it.

They took it for granted.

Over the last couple of years, I've been changing that.

I've stopped chasing people. Stopped accepting behavior I once tolerated. Stopped making myself responsible for relationships that apparently cannot exist without my constant effort.

And something fascinating, and rather fucking painful, happened.

**When I stopped reaching out, very few people reached back.**

## The brothers I never thought I'd lose

I have two younger brothers.

Growing up, when Elizabeth wasn't there, Martin and I helped take care of Yavor.

We were there for him.

Decades later, Yavor has decided that loyalty to Elizabeth is a condition of having a relationship with me.

He actually suggested that perhaps we could have a relationship after our mother dies.

Think about that.

Two brothers, both adults, both capable of deciding what kind of relationship they want with each other.

And apparently we have to wait for somebody to fucking die before we can have one.

No.

I spent two years before responding to one of his emails. When I finally did, I addressed his points. I wasn't asking for a reunion. I wasn't asking him to choose sides.

And somehow the conversation became about loyalty to Elizabeth, with Martin dragged into it.

I had to ask him something.

Where the fuck were you when we were growing up? Who took care of you when Elizabeth wasn't there?

Martin did.

I did.

And now those brothers are somehow the problem.

Fine.

You have made your choice.

And I have made mine.

## The hardest goodbye

Martin is different.

And this is the one that hurts.

I know Martin cares about me. I know he's trying. I know he's learning, growing, and doing the best he can with what he understands.

I'm genuinely happy for him.

But I've spent decades trying to be the good older brother. Helping him, supporting him, hoping we could have the kind of relationship I wanted.

And now, finally, he wants that relationship.

The tragedy is that **I'm no longer the person who can give it to him.**

I've changed.

And I don't think he really sees that.

He still approaches me through the lens of the person he's known for thirty years.

Our conversations remain superficial. He asks how I'm doing, I tell him, and then he moves past what I've said. It's happened more than once.

And I find myself wondering why we're even having the conversation.

He returns to old patterns, old judgments, my drinking, the things he remembers about me.

But what about the person sitting in front of him today?

I don't need another lecture.

I don't need another conversation about who I was.

I need someone who gives a shit about who I am.

Eventually, I wrote him an email.

The subject was *Saying Goodbye*.

I told him I didn't need a break. I couldn't have him in my life anymore.

And I told him something that hurt immensely just to write.

I knew he'd made an effort. I knew he was trying. But I'd given him chances, and I'd been disappointed too many times.

I wished him peace and happiness.

I asked him not to contact me again.

And as I was writing that goodbye, Queen's *The Show Must Go On* was playing.

Of all the fucking songs.

I ended the email:

**"I have suffered too much. And Queen is playing right on time... The Show Must Go On. Goodbye my brother."**

Jesus Christ.

I never thought I'd write those words.

And I cannot pretend they don't hurt.

## Thirty-five years of friendship

Then there's my old high-school friend.

We met when we were fifteen. Thirty-five years ago.

In 2013, I helped bring him to the United States. I put him up for a year, paid his expenses, helped him get established.

He eventually built a life in Arizona.

Good for him.

Years later, he admitted he'd been holding resentment toward me over a woman he believed I'd somehow stolen from him.

Stolen.

As if another human being had no agency of her own.

As if I'd put her in a fucking bag and carried her away.

And apparently he'd been holding on to this for years, even while supposedly trying to rebuild our friendship.

Eventually I got tired of maintaining the relationship.

I stopped reaching out.

It's been six months.

He has my email. He knows how to contact me.

Nothing.

And here's the thing.

I don't want my money back. I don't want gratitude for everything I did. I don't need some elaborate acknowledgment of my generosity.

**I just wanted somebody to give a shit about me.**

To ask how I'm doing.

To remember I exist without needing something from me.

Apparently that was too much to hope for in this relationship.

## Some problems are not meant to be solved

Recently I heard a philosophical idea, associated with Carl Jung, that has stayed with me:

**Some problems are not meant to be solved. They are meant to be outgrown.**

Elizabeth is one of those problems.

For years, I tried to understand, explain, confront, repair, and resolve our relationship.

I wanted accountability. I wanted acknowledgment. I wanted her to understand what had happened and what it had cost me.

I even wrote her a long email confronting the past. Asking where she had been when we were growing up. Reminding her who had actually taken care of whom.

Her response?

I was angry.

And she hoped I'd find a woman to fill the space my mother had left in my life.

Jesus fucking Christ.

Even then, the conversation wasn't about what I'd said. It was about my reaction to it.

And after all these years, I've arrived at a rather extraordinary realization.

I cannot solve Elizabeth.

There is nothing I can do to make her become the person I once needed her to be.

And I no longer need to.

I've outgrown that relationship.

At least, I thought I had.

Because there's one thing that still hurts.

Her influence on the people around her. The relationships that become tangled up in old loyalties, expectations, and family history.

I feel sadness for my brothers.

And sometimes I'm frustrated with myself for still caring so much.

Because that's the part of me that hasn't changed.

**I still care about these people.**

I want them to be happy.

I don't want them to suffer.

But I cannot keep caring for them at the expense of myself.

I've done that for fifty fucking years.

No more.

## Compassion without attachment

Somewhere in all this, I've been exploring Buddhism, Zen, and the idea of non-attachment.

Not indifference.

Not pretending people don't matter.

Not turning myself into some emotionless fucking statue.

But recognizing that compassion doesn't require possession, obligation, or endless self-sacrifice.

There is a Buddhist practice called *Mettā*, loving-kindness meditation.

A practice of wishing happiness, peace, and freedom from suffering for others.

Including people who have hurt you.

Including people who are no longer in your life.

And that resonates with me enormously.

Because despite everything I've said tonight, despite the anger and the hurt, I don't actually want any of these people to suffer.

Not Martin.

Not Yavor.

Not Elizabeth.

Not even the friend in Arizona.

I want them to find whatever it is they're looking for.

I want them to find happiness.

I want them to find peace.

I simply cannot keep being the person who tries to provide those things for them.

And perhaps there's an important distinction between loving somebody and carrying them.

I can love somebody without carrying their problems.

I can acknowledge their efforts without offering another chance.

I can wish them happiness without participating in their lives.

And I can say goodbye without wishing them harm.

## And then, Numb

I thought I'd reached the end of what I needed to say.

I had said goodbye to my brother.

I'd confronted the past, acknowledged the pain, and finally found the words to wish these people peace.

Queen had been playing when I wrote that goodbye.

*The Show Must Go On.*

How fucking appropriate.

And now, while I'm putting fifty years of my life into words, another song comes on.

Linkin Park.

*Numb.*

...

Oh, you've got to be FUCKING kidding me.

Of all the songs.

Of all the fucking moments.

*Numb.*

A song about the crushing weight of expectations. About being trapped inside somebody else's idea of who you're supposed to be. About the exhaustion of trying to become what other people demand while slowly losing the person you actually are.

And suddenly I'm sitting here thinking about the last fifty years.

The older brother.

The provider.

The problem solver.

The dependable friend.

The man who always showed up.

The man who always gave more.

The man who always put everybody else first.

**And where the fuck was I in all of this?**

Where was the person looking after me?

Where was the person asking what I needed?

And perhaps the most painful question of all:

**Why did I spend so much of my life believing that everybody else's happiness mattered more than my own?**

Jesus Christ.

I've spent years trying to explain to people that I've changed.

That I'm not the person they remember.

That I cannot keep playing the role they assigned me decades ago.

And they keep looking at me as if nothing has happened.

As if I'm still supposed to be that person.

As if the last fifty years belong to them.

But they don't.

They belong to me.

And here's the fucking kicker.

I don't want to stop caring about these people.

I never wanted to stop loving them.

**I just wanted to stop disappearing inside the act of loving them.**

And there it is.

Queen was playing when I said goodbye to my brother.

Linkin Park is playing as I realize what I've been saying goodbye to all along.

**The version of myself that I sacrificed to keep everyone else happy.**

One song for the people I'm leaving behind.

Another for the person I'm finally allowing myself to become.

Fucking hell.

I couldn't have scripted this if I tried.

## In the End

And then, just as I'm sitting here contemplating how absurdly appropriate *Numb* was, the next song starts.

Linkin Park.

Again.

*In the End.*

...

Oh, FUCK OFF, universe.

Seriously?

**IN THE FUCKING END?**

You couldn't have picked something cheerful? Some ridiculous eighties pop song? A little disco? Something about sunshine and dancing?

No.

Of course not.

You give me *In the End*.

A song about time passing. About effort. About pouring yourself into something and discovering that, despite everything you invested, things didn't turn out the way you hoped.

Well, isn't that just fucking perfect?

Because that's precisely what I've been confronting tonight.

Thirty-five years of friendship.

Fifty years of family.

Decades of taking care of people.

Decades of believing that if I just kept showing up, kept trying, kept caring, eventually people would see me.

Eventually they would understand.

Eventually the relationships would become what I'd hoped they could be.

And here I am.

Fifty years old.

Writing goodbye emails to my brothers.

No longer speaking to my mother.

Watching a thirty-five-year friendship disappear into silence.

And listening to Linkin Park.

Again.

But here's where I part company with the despair that could so easily come from looking back at all those years.

**Just because something didn't become what I hoped doesn't mean the years I gave it were meaningless.**

I loved these people.

I cared.

I showed up.

I did what I believed was right.

Sometimes I did too much.

Sometimes I gave more than I should have.

Sometimes I accepted behavior I should have walked away from years earlier.

And sometimes I made mistakes.

Plenty of fucking mistakes.

But I was there.

I cannot change that history.

And I don't need to condemn myself for having cared.

What I can do is recognize that the person I am today no longer has to repeat it.

I don't need to spend another fifty years proving my love through sacrifice.

I don't need to spend another fifty years explaining myself to people who still see the version of me they remember.

And I don't need to spend another fifty years trying to solve relationships that cannot be solved by me alone.

**It mattered. All of it mattered.**

But I matter, too.

And for perhaps the first time in my life, I'm willing to act accordingly.

## The universe has a sense of humor

Just look at this fucking sequence.

First, Queen's *The Show Must Go On*, playing while I write what may be the hardest goodbye of my life.

Then Linkin Park's *Numb*, playing while I confront fifty years of living according to other people's expectations.

And immediately afterward, Linkin Park's *In the End*, arriving just as I'm reflecting on decades of effort, sacrifice, and relationships that didn't become what I hoped.

Three songs.

In that order.

On this particular night.

You cannot make this shit up.

Well, you probably could, but nobody would believe you.

And here I am, the same man who spent the evening laughing at Bruce Willis and John Malkovich, now sitting here contemplating Buddhism, Jung, family, forgiveness, and the extraordinary absurdity of being alive.

I sometimes wonder what the fuck I did in a previous life.

Because this one has been one hell of a ride.

And apparently the universe has decided that simply throwing mechanical disasters, family conflicts, legal nonsense, and existential questions at me isn't quite enough.

It also needs to DJ the experience.

And the bastard has impeccable timing.

**The universe surely has a sense of humor.**

And I can still laugh.

Not because none of this hurts.

Not because I've somehow transcended suffering and become an enlightened Zen master sitting on a fucking mountain.

But because even in the middle of some of the most painful decisions I've ever made, life remains spectacularly, gloriously absurd.

And perhaps that's enough for tonight.

## May you find your peace

I'm fifty.

I've had an extraordinarily absurd life.

Some of it has been wonderful. Some of it has been painful. Some of it has been so fucking ridiculous that I sometimes wonder whether I'm the protagonist of a cosmic practical joke.

But here I am.

Still laughing.

Still fascinated by philosophy.

Still learning.

Still trying to understand what it means to live peacefully.

And still capable of caring deeply about people I've decided I can no longer have in my life.

I don't regret being generous.

I don't regret caring.

But I do regret how often I neglected myself in the process.

I cannot change the fifty years behind me.

And I cannot make other people recognize the person I've become.

What I can do is stop demanding that the past become something different.

I don't need to resolve every relationship.

I don't need everyone to understand.

And I don't need to keep explaining myself.

For Martin, for Yavor, for Elizabeth, and for the people I've loved and left behind, I have one final wish.

Not a demand.

Not an invitation.

Not an attempt to reconcile.

Just a simple blessing.

> May you find your peace.
>
> May you find your happiness.
>
> And I wish you well.

And perhaps, finally, I can offer that same kindness to myself.

**No more sacrificing myself to keep other people in my life.**

I can care deeply.

I can let go completely.

And I can wish them well as we walk our separate paths.

After fifty years, perhaps that's the closest I've come to understanding what peace actually means.

Not the absence of suffering.

Not the resolution of every conflict.

Not the perfect family, friendship, or relationship.

Just the freedom to stop carrying what was never mine to carry forever.

And now?

Well...

Queen already said it.

**The show must go on.**

And so shall I.
