*Lucifer, Discount Store Marcus Aurelius, and the apparently terrifying art of asking a fucking question.*

I have apparently discovered a new form of psychological warfare.

It's called **asking a straightforward question.**

Terrifying stuff.

<!-- truncate -->

I've always heard that Bulgarians are direct. Blunt, even. They don't dance around things. They tell you exactly what they think, occasionally before you've had the opportunity to ask.

Wonderful, I thought.

Finally, my people.

Except there's Bulgarian directness.

There's ordinary human directness.

And then there's **my brand of direct**, which apparently requires a government permit, two witnesses, and a fucking psychiatric evaluation.

Allow me to explain.

## The Crime

About a week ago, an 18-year-old Bulgarian girl liked me on a dating app.

Now, before anyone gets excited, I had absolutely no expectations of this going anywhere. She's eighteen. I'm fifty. I wasn't planning a wedding, selecting curtains, or preparing to introduce her to my imaginary grandchildren.

She contacted me.

"Hey. Zdravei. Hello."

Marvelous.

Human communication has commenced.

I responded with something truly outrageous.

"Hello, how are you?"

I know.

Reckless.

Somewhere, a relationship counselor just dropped their fucking clipboard.

And then...

Nothing.

Complete silence.

Her profile contained approximately as much information as a Soviet-era government document after declassification.

Which is to say, practically fuck-all.

No interesting description. No conversational hooks. Nothing to suggest what she liked, disliked, or did with her existence.

Even Sherlock Holmes would have looked at the evidence and said, "Well, fuck me, Watson. We're going to need a second clue."

So I waited.

Not because I was emotionally invested. I had simply moved on with my life, as one generally does when a conversation consists of two greetings and an unsolved mystery.

A week passed.

## The Audacity

Today, I decided to ask a question.

My standard opening, which is rapidly becoming my personal trademark:

**"I don't mean to be an asshole, but..."**

Now, I find this increasingly hilarious because apparently the mere act of announcing a straightforward question is sufficient to qualify me as an asshole.

What followed was particularly scandalous.

"Are you still interested in talking?"

That's it.

No accusations.

No emotional manifesto.

No three-page dissertation on the decline of Western civilization.

Just a question.

And within moments...

**UNMATCHED.**

HAHAHAHA!

A week to not answer "How are you?"

Approximately three seconds to answer "Do you still want to talk?"

Ladies and gentlemen, we've discovered a new form of communication.

The answer is conveyed entirely through the emergency evacuation procedure.

## Enter Lucifer

Somewhere in the theatrical department of my imagination, Lucifer was laughing so hard he nearly spilled his drink.

"Darling," he said, "you can't simply ask people what they want."

"Why the fuck not?"

"Because then they might have to know."

"She contacted me!"

"Yes."

"She initiated the conversation!"

"Indeed."

"And then she couldn't even answer 'How are you?'"

"Apparently not."

"So I asked whether she wanted to continue."

Lucifer considered this.

"Oh, you magnificent bastard. You demanded *clarity*. From a dating app. That's like demanding accountability from a Bulgarian government office."

I had to admit he had a point.

"You see," Lucifer continued, "you've misunderstood the entire arrangement. You're supposed to perform a little dance. Send something witty. Interpret the silence. Invent a personality from three photographs. Perhaps consult the stars."

"I don't do interpretive dance."

"Clearly, darling. You prefer interrogating the orchestra about whether the concert is still happening."

## Discount Store Marcus Aurelius Intervenes

At this point, my internal Stoic philosopher appeared.

Not the actual Marcus Aurelius, obviously.

The actual Marcus Aurelius was an emperor, philosopher, and military commander.

Mine came from the discount aisle at Lidl.

**Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.**

Same philosophy. Questionable manufacturing standards.

"Remember," he announced solemnly, "you cannot control the actions of others. You can control only your own response."

"Yes, Marcus. I'm familiar with Stoicism."

"Then why concern yourself with an unmatch?"

"I'm not concerned. I'm fucking laughing."

"Ah. Then you have achieved tranquility."

"I've achieved comedy, Marcus. There's a difference."

He adjusted his imaginary toga.

"Perhaps the girl simply changed her mind."

"Entirely possible."

"Perhaps she was uninterested."

"Also possible."

"Perhaps she found your opening abrasive."

"Certainly possible. And notice how I haven't demanded that the Roman Senate convene an investigation."

He nodded approvingly.

"Then you have learned to accept what is beyond your control."

"Marcus, I've been practicing this shit for years. I'm not disturbed by the result. I'm entertained by the method."

He contemplated this for several seconds.

"Then perhaps laughter is your meditation."

"Perhaps you should go back to Lidl."

## The Bulgarian Paradox

Here's what really amuses me.

Bulgarians have a reputation for being remarkably direct.

They'll tell you your haircut looks ridiculous, your cooking is terrible, and your life decisions are questionable, sometimes before you've even finished saying good morning.

And yet, in my dating-app adventures, I've encountered people for whom a direct question seems to present an extraordinary conversational challenge.

Of course, I can't know why this particular girl unmatched me.

Maybe she wasn't interested. Maybe my wording rubbed her the wrong way. Maybe she'd already forgotten the match existed.

Fair enough.

But there's something deliciously absurd about a culture associated with bluntness meeting a man who apparently takes bluntness to its logical conclusion.

"Are we talking or not?"

**GOOD LORD, MAN! THINK OF THE CHILDREN!**

Lucifer, naturally, was delighted.

"Darling, you've managed to make directness look indirect."

"How?"

"You started with 'I don't mean to be an asshole.'"

"That's politeness!"

"No, that's a theatrical announcement that an asshole may be arriving shortly."

"Well, at least I'm providing advance notice."

"How very considerate."

## The Roman Emoji Purge

Ah, but I forgot to mention my other method.

My favorite, actually, because it doesn't even require someone to type a word.

A match appears.

Instead of "Hello," I receive:

👋

A waving hand.

An entire civilization invented language, poetry, literature, philosophy, and the fucking internet so a grown adult could send me a digital hand.

And this asshole, the one writing this very story, has a standard response:

**"You will have to do better than an emoji."**

That's it.

I didn't ask for Shakespeare. I didn't demand a dissertation. I didn't require three references and a notarized declaration of conversational competence.

I asked for something resembling a sentence.

And apparently, by my thoroughly unscientific estimate, approximately **99% immediately unmatch me.**

GLORIOUS!

Lucifer nearly fell out of his chair.

"Darling, you've invented conversational population control."

"I prefer quality assurance."

"Someone waves at you, and you respond with a performance review?"

"Precisely."

"You're aware that the wave is supposed to be an invitation to conversation?"

"Then fucking converse!"

"Perhaps they expect you to initiate."

"She matched with me, sent a picture of a hand, and now I'm expected to perform the entire fucking interaction?"

Lucifer raised his glass.

"You really are an extraordinary pain in the ass."

"Thank you."

"That wasn't a compliment."

"I've decided to accept it as one."

### The Emperor Objects

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius emerged from the clearance aisle, wearing a toga that still had the Lidl security tag attached.

"Ben, a wise man does not judge another person by a single gesture."

"Marcus, it's not a judgment. It's a filter."

"Perhaps they are shy."

"Perfectly possible."

"Perhaps they are interesting people who struggle with opening conversations."

"Also possible."

"Then you may be dismissing someone worthy of your attention."

"Indeed. And they may be dismissing the greatest conversational experience of their lives. Tragedies everywhere, Marcus."

The emperor sighed.

"You cannot control how others communicate."

"Correct. But I can control whether I participate."

"And what happens when they unmatch?"

"I laugh."

"Then you accept the consequences?"

"With fucking enthusiasm."

Lucifer stood, lifted his imaginary goblet, and proclaimed:

**"FOR THE GLORY OF ROME!"**

"FOR THE GLORY OF ROME!" I replied.

Marcus looked deeply concerned.

"Rome eventually fell, you know."

"Yes, Marcus. But not because someone refused to engage with a fucking waving emoji."

And thus another match disappeared into the digital wilderness.

No casualties.

No broken hearts.

No unnecessary correspondence.

Just one waving hand, one straightforward sentence, and another magnificent demonstration of my brand of direct.

**The Roman Empire may be gone, but its border controls are alive and well on my dating profile.**

---

## The Recurring Circus

And this isn't the first time.

That's what makes it funny.

There's a recurring little performance in modern dating.

Somebody matches.

Somebody says hello.

A conversation may or may not begin.

Then, occasionally, the entire thing simply evaporates.

No drama. No explanation. Just the conversational equivalent of an abandoned shopping trolley.

And sometimes, when I ask the obvious question, I receive an answer in the form of an exit.

It's almost elegant.

The dating app has accidentally invented a perfectly efficient binary communication protocol.

**Interested?**

- Yes: Maybe a reply.
- No: Disappearance.
- Error: Connection terminated by remote host.

As a software engineer, I have to respect the efficiency.

As a human being, I have to laugh at the absurdity.

And as Lucifer would undoubtedly point out, even Hell has the courtesy to acknowledge receipt of a soul.

## The Stoic Conclusion

Discount Marcus returned for one final observation.

"Do not attach yourself to the outcome."

"I'm not attached."

"Do not demand a response."

"I didn't demand one."

"Do not surrender your peace to another person's actions."

"Marcus, for fuck's sake. I'm laughing."

He paused.

Then, with all the wisdom available to a philosopher purchased at 40% off, he said:

"Then there is nothing to resolve."

Precisely.

I wasn't expecting anything to come of that match.

I didn't lose anything.

I asked a question. The other person made a choice. Life continued.

And now I have another absurd little story to add to the collection.

Perhaps that's my particular brand of directness.

I don't particularly enjoy guessing what somebody means when I can simply ask.

Sometimes I get an answer.

Sometimes I get silence.

And occasionally I get an unmatch delivered with the urgency of a nuclear launch sequence.

All perfectly acceptable outcomes.

Besides, I've finally discovered my epitaph:

**HERE LIES BEN.**

*He didn't mean to be an asshole.*

*He just wanted to know whether the fucking conversation was still happening.*

Lucifer raised his glass.

Discount Marcus nodded.

And somewhere in Bulgaria, an unanswered "How are you?" remained the most dangerous question of the week.

**Consequences!**
