# You'll Get Used to It — My Brother Mario

I didn't plan to write about Mario tonight.

I was remembering all kinds of ridiculous shit from my life, going back thirty years, talking about people I'd met, places I'd been, things I'd done.

And somehow I ended up remembering Mario.

And now I'm sitting here laughing and crying about this magnificent motherfucker.

Because Mario was one of the most amazing human beings I've ever had the pleasure of knowing.

And I fucking loved him.

<!-- truncate -->

## Who the Fuck Is This Guy?

Mario was the former husband of my mother's sister.

They'd been divorced for something like forty years.

He was the father of my cousins.

Technically, I suppose, he was my uncle.

But that's not who Mario was to me.

**Mario was my fucking brother.**

And the funniest part is that neither one of us expected that relationship to happen.

I remember him telling me about the first time we met.

Apparently, I showed up somewhere wearing a suit.

I used to wear suits when I went out. That's just how I dressed.

And Mario's first impression was something along the lines of:

> Who the fuck is this motherfucker showing up in a suit?

😂

I still laugh thinking about that.

Here was this little guy, maybe five-foot-two, looking at some asshole dressed in a suit, wondering who the hell he was.

And somehow, somewhere along the way, we became friends.

Not just friends.

Brothers.

We resonated on the same fucking frequency.

I cannot explain exactly how that happened.

It just did.

## Five Feet Two of Pure Fucking Life

Mario was a character.

Jesus fucking Christ, what a character.

The motherfucker was maybe five-foot-two, but everywhere we went, he was the life of the goddamn party.

Everywhere.

He loved people.

He loved having a good time.

He loved being alive.

And I fucking loved being around him.

We traveled together.

We flew places together.

We went out.

We drank.

We laughed.

We did all kinds of ridiculous shit.

Some of the best times I've had with another human being in the last several years of his life were with Mario.

And none of this had anything to do with being related.

I didn't spend time with Mario because he was my aunt's ex-husband.

I spent time with Mario because I genuinely fucking loved the guy.

He understood me.

I understood him.

We didn't need to explain ourselves to each other.

We could simply be ourselves.

And that is something I've rarely experienced with another human being at that level.

No judgment.

No lectures.

No expectations about who I should be.

Just two fucking guys enjoying each other's company.

And I cannot express how grateful I am that life brought this ridiculous little motherfucker into mine.

## You'll Get Used to It

Mario didn't have much money.

His pension was something like €300 a month.

I had money.

So when we went places, I paid.

Dinner, drinks, whatever the fuck.

It didn't matter to me.

I wasn't keeping track.

I wasn't trying to impress anybody.

I was spending time with my friend.

And my time with Mario was worth far more to me than whatever the bill happened to be.

One day, he said something to me.

He told me he wasn't comfortable with me spending so much money on him.

I looked at him.

And without even thinking, I said:

> **You'll get used to it.**

That was it.

😂😂😂

Jesus fucking Christ.

I still laugh my ass off remembering that conversation.

I didn't plan that response.

I didn't think about it.

I wasn't trying to be clever.

The words just came out.

**You'll get used to it.**

What the fuck was he supposed to say to that?

And here's the funniest part.

He fucking did.

We never had that conversation again.

Not once.

Mario got used to it.

And we kept going places, spending time together, enjoying life.

Money is money.

People are people.

And Mario was worth every fucking minute I spent with him.

I don't remember what those dinners cost.

I remember Mario.

And that's what matters to me.

## The Man Who Laughed at Cancer

Mario had cancer.

He fought it.

He lost his hair.

And the motherfucker joked about it.

Because that's who Mario was.

He could find something to laugh about even when life was throwing some seriously ugly shit at him.

And then the cancer was gone.

He'd beaten it.

And then it came back.

I knew he was suffering.

I saw it.

I was there.

And Mario knew he was going to die.

He wasn't under some illusion that he was going to live forever.

He joked about death.

He talked about it.

He understood what was happening.

And as far as I could see, he took it in stride.

He continued being Mario.

The same fucking character who loved life.

Cancer didn't suddenly turn him into somebody else.

And that is something I admired enormously about him.

I remember being in his apartment when he was sick.

He was lying in bed.

I was there with him.

And he squeezed my hand.

I will never fucking forget that.

Never.

I knew this man.

I knew how much he was suffering.

And I knew that eventually he was going to go.

We all go.

That's life.

There is nothing extraordinary about dying.

What matters is what we do with the time we have.

And Mario, that magnificent motherfucker, knew how to fucking live.

## The Funeral I Couldn't Bear

And then Mario died.

I knew it was coming.

He knew it was coming.

His cancer had returned, and he had suffered terribly.

I hoped he had finally found some peace.

Then came the funeral.

And this is the part that still tears me up.

They buried Mario in a church.

There were priests.

There was a coffin.

There was all this sorrow.

And I walked in and saw his body lying there.

I couldn't fucking take it.

I felt physically sick.

Like I was going to throw up.

I couldn't stay.

I walked out, got into my car, and left.

And I never went back.

Because to me, everything about that funeral felt completely wrong for the Mario I knew.

**This was not how I believed Mario would have wanted to be remembered.**

Where was the laughter?

Where were the stories?

Where was the celebration of this magnificent human being who had loved life so fucking much?

Why weren't we getting together, having some drinks, laughing about the ridiculous things he'd done?

Why weren't we telling stories about this little five-foot-two motherfucker who could make an entire room come alive?

I wanted a fucking party for Mario.

I wanted people laughing their asses off remembering what an amazing character he'd been.

Instead, I saw a coffin and a room full of sorrow.

And it broke something in me.

I know people mourn in different ways.

I know his daughter and his friends had their own relationships with him.

But I knew Mario, too.

I knew him intimately.

I loved the man.

And that ceremony felt so disconnected from everything I knew about him that I simply couldn't be there.

I couldn't explain that to anybody.

I didn't believe they would understand.

And I wasn't going to stand there telling people how to grieve or how to bury their father.

That wasn't my place.

So I left.

And to this day, I have no desire to visit his grave.

Because that's not where Mario exists for me.

**Mario exists in the fucking memories.**

In the laughter.

In the adventures.

In the time we spent together.

In that little motherfucker looking at me in a suit and wondering who the hell I was.

And in four ridiculous words that still make me laugh.

*You'll get used to it.*

## My Brother, Not My Uncle

What makes this whole thing even more absurd is how Mario and I came to know each other.

We weren't childhood friends.

We didn't grow up together.

We weren't brothers by blood.

He was my aunt's ex-husband.

The father of my cousins.

A man I had absolutely no reason to expect would become one of the closest people in my life.

And somehow he did.

That's one of the things I've come to appreciate about life.

You never fucking know.

You meet somebody.

You have a conversation.

You spend some time together.

And suddenly, without planning any of it, you've found somebody who understands you in a way you never expected.

Mario was that person for me.

We didn't need to define the relationship.

We didn't need to explain it.

We just fucking lived it.

And I know his family may never understand how close we were.

That's okay.

**Mario knew. And I knew.**

That's enough.

## What I Wish I Could Tell You

Mario, my brother.

I fucking loved you.

I loved spending time with you.

I loved traveling with you.

I loved laughing with you.

I loved every ridiculous fucking adventure we had together.

And I am so goddamn glad I got to know you.

You were an amazing human being.

Not because you were perfect.

Not because you were some great philosopher or extraordinary intellectual.

Because you were Mario.

A little five-foot-two motherfucker who loved life and knew how to enjoy it.

And you made my life better simply by being in it.

I wish we'd had more time.

Of course I do.

But I don't need to pretend that the time we had wasn't enough to mean something.

We had our time.

We enjoyed it.

We were brothers.

And then you were gone.

That's life.

I accept that.

But I wish your farewell had been different.

I wish we'd celebrated you the way I remember you celebrating life.

And I'm sorry I couldn't give you that.

I'm sorry I walked out of that church.

I couldn't bear seeing you that way.

Not you, brother.

Not my fucking Mario.

And yet, every time I remember you, I smile.

Every fucking time.

Even now, with tears running down my face, I cannot think about you without laughing at some ridiculous thing we did.

And I wouldn't trade those memories for anything.

## Peace, My Brother

I've been thinking a lot lately about Stoicism, Zen, impermanence, life and death.

And Mario, you keep reminding me of something I've always believed.

**What makes life precious is that it doesn't last forever.**

We don't get to keep the people we love indefinitely.

We don't get to stop time.

We don't get to demand another twenty years.

We get whatever time we get.

And what matters to me is being present for it.

Enjoying it.

Appreciating the people we're with.

Telling them how much they mean to us while they're still here.

And that's exactly what I did with you.

I loved spending time with you.

I told you.

I showed up.

We laughed.

We traveled.

We had our adventures.

And now I have those memories.

I don't need to turn your death into some grand tragedy to acknowledge how much you meant to me.

I'm happy I knew you.

I'm fucking delighted that life gave me the opportunity to be your friend.

And I'm grateful beyond words that somehow you became my brother.

I don't know where these memories will take me in another ten, fifteen, or twenty years.

Maybe I'll be sitting somewhere reading this and laughing my fucking ass off all over again.

Maybe I'll remember another ridiculous thing you said.

Maybe I'll cry.

I don't know.

But I know this.

**I will never fucking forget you.**

Not because of how you died.

Not because of your funeral.

But because of how you lived.

Because of the person you were.

Because of the friendship we shared.

And because of one of the funniest, most spontaneous things I've ever said to another human being.

You told me you weren't comfortable with me paying for you.

And I looked at you and said:

> **You'll get used to it.**

And you fucking did.

😂

Goddamn, Mario.

What a fucking character.

What a fucking man.

What an amazing human being.

Thank you for being in my life.

Thank you for the laughter.

Thank you for the memories.

Thank you for being my brother.

**Peace, my brother Mario.**

I hope you found yours.

And wherever you are, I hope you're laughing.

Because every time I remember you, I fucking am.
