# Lucifer Has Discontinued Customer Retention

Lucifer was tired.

Not of Hell. Hell was relatively predictable. People arrived, complained about their circumstances, blamed somebody else, and occasionally demanded to speak to management.

Humanity, however, was becoming increasingly exhausting.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Woman from Miami

One evening, Lucifer received a message on Telegram.

> "How are you, James?"

Now, Lucifer had been called many things throughout his existence.

Satan. The Devil. Morningstar. The Prince of Darkness.

But James was a fucking first.

The woman introduced herself, sent a photograph, and apologized for contacting the wrong person. Nevertheless, she was delighted that they had met.

> "May I know your name?"

"Lucifer."

> "Nice name."

Lucifer stared at the screen.

*Not even a fucking follow-up question.*

Within thirty minutes, a woman who hadn't known he existed was explaining that the Atlantic Ocean shouldn't be an obstacle if he genuinely wanted to get to know her.

Lucifer, having encountered this particular species of human interaction before, asked a rather inconvenient question.

"How do I know you're not a scammer?"

Somewhere, potentially in a poorly ventilated office on another continent, an employee discovered that the Prince of Darkness required identity verification.

## Meanwhile, in Bulgaria

A few days earlier, Lucifer had invited a woman out.

Good food, wine, conversation. Nothing particularly complicated.

She preferred coffee or lunch, was unavailable that weekend, and then asked about his position on paying for first dates.

Lucifer explained that he had always paid, that there was no rush, and that since he had already extended an invitation, the next one was hers.

He wasn't going to chase her.

She unmatched him.

And so Lucifer found himself contemplating a remarkable geographical phenomenon:

A woman supposedly in Miami was prepared to overcome an ocean, while arranging an evening with someone considerably closer seemed to require diplomatic negotiations.

## The Department of Human Acquisition

God looked down at his son.

"Have you considered being a little less direct?"

"Father, I've considered it."

"And?"

"I've discontinued customer retention."

God raised an eyebrow.

"Customer retention?"

"Yes. The department responsible for maintaining somebody's interest through strategic ambiguity, repeated invitations, carefully timed messages, and pretending I give a shit when I don't."

"And what happens when people leave?"

"They leave."

"Doesn't that bother you?"

"No."

God remained silent.

Lucifer continued.

"If somebody wants to spend time with me, wonderful. If they don't, equally wonderful. I have things to build, philosophy to contemplate, music to listen to, and a dog who has never asked me about my position on splitting the dinner bill."

## Lucifer Is Tired

There was a time when Lucifer might have entertained the conversation, explained himself further, or attempted to establish whether somebody was genuinely interested.

These days, he simply said what he meant.

Sometimes people appreciated it.

Sometimes they disappeared.

Sometimes they attempted to introduce him to the exciting world of cryptocurrency investment.

The outcome was no longer particularly interesting.

Lucifer poured himself a coffee and returned to his work.

For the first time in several thousand years, Hell had no marketing department, no customer acquisition strategy, and absolutely no interest in improving its conversion rate.

Business had never been more peaceful.
