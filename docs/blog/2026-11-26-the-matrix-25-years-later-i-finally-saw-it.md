# The Matrix: 25 Years Later, I Finally Saw It

Lucifer sat across from God, looking deeply offended.

"Father, I have a complaint."

God sighed. "Another one?"

"I have spent twenty-five fucking years watching *The Matrix*. Twenty-five years. I know every line. Every scene. Every ridiculous little philosophical exchange."

"And?"

"And apparently I've been watching the fucking Bible."

<!-- truncate -->

## In the Beginning, There Was Piracy

It was the late 1990s, or perhaps the early 2000s.

The internet was slow. Hard drives were small. And pirating a movie required considerably more dedication than clicking a button.

My first pirated movie was *The Matrix*.

On Video CDs.

VCDs, for those fortunate enough never to have experienced this technological atrocity, were CDs containing MPEG video compressed to approximately the visual quality of a potato.

A feature film generally required two discs.

Halfway through the movie, you'd get up, swap the disc, and hope the second one wasn't corrupted.

We watched that fucking movie so many times I could practically recite the screenplay.

"I know kung fu."

"Show me."

"What is real?"

"There is no spoon."

I knew the dialogue. I knew the characters. I knew the story.

Or so I thought.

## Twenty-Five Years Later

One day, I watched Laurence Fishburne talking with Neil deGrasse Tyson about *The Matrix*.

And Fishburne discussed the biblical interpretation of the story.

Morpheus as John the Baptist.

Neo as Jesus.

The prophecy. The chosen one. The sacrifice. The resurrection.

And suddenly something clicked.

Lucifer slowly turned toward God.

"Father."

"Yes?"

"Are you telling me that Morpheus has been John the fucking Baptist this entire time?"

God smiled.

"And Neo?"

"Keep going."

"And the resurrection?"

"You're getting there."

"And the ship is called *Nebuchadnezzar*?"

"Very good."

Lucifer stared into the distance.

"Twenty-five fucking years."

God nodded.

"Twenty-five years."

"I have watched this film so many times I can quote practically every line."

"Apparently."

"And somehow I never connected all of that."

God shrugged.

"There's a difference between knowing the words and recognizing the story."

Lucifer looked disgusted.

"Don't you dare turn this into a fucking sermon."

## Enter Discount Store Marcus Aurelius

At this point, Discount Store Marcus Aurelius appeared, wearing a bedsheet and carrying a coffee mug that said *Memento Mori, But First Coffee*.

"I believe," he announced, "that this demonstrates the importance of perception."

Lucifer closed his eyes.

"Oh, for fuck's sake."

"As Epictetus taught us, men are disturbed not by things, but by their judgments about things."

"Marcus, we're discussing a science-fiction movie."

"Indeed. And is the spoon truly there?"

"That's a different philosopher, you discount-store bedsheet."

Marcus nodded thoughtfully.

"Perhaps the true Matrix was the friends we made along the way."

Lucifer threw a biscuit at him.

## And Then Laurence Fishburne Entered My Dreams

As if that revelation weren't strange enough, I subsequently had a dream.

About Laurence Fishburne.

Not Morpheus.

Laurence fucking Fishburne.

Apparently, in this dream, I knew him personally.

Somewhere in upstate New York.

Why upstate New York?

No fucking idea.

Why Laurence Fishburne?

Again, not a clue.

But my sleeping brain had apparently decided that after twenty-five years of watching this man explain reality, I should finally meet him socially.

God looked amused.

"So you dreamed about Morpheus."

"No, Father. I dreamed about Laurence Fishburne. We knew each other."

"Perhaps he was trying to show you the truth."

"Perhaps my brain was just being a fucking idiot."

Discount Marcus raised one finger.

"Dreams are but shadows upon the cave wall."

"That's Plato, Marcus."

"Philosophy is a collaborative discipline."

## The Joke

Here's the part I find genuinely fascinating.

For a quarter of a century, I knew this movie intimately.

I didn't need someone to explain the plot.

I didn't need a recap.

And yet, one conversation revealed a layer I had somehow overlooked.

Not because the information was unavailable.

Not because the symbolism was particularly well hidden.

It had simply never occurred to me to look at the film that way.

And that, perhaps, is the most entertaining part of the whole thing.

You can spend twenty-five years knowing something and still discover that you've never seen one of its most interesting dimensions.

Lucifer looked at God.

"So what's the lesson, Father?"

God smiled.

"Must there be one?"

Lucifer considered this.

Then smiled back.

"No. I suppose there fucking doesn't."

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius cleared his throat.

"Nevertheless, the obstacle is the way."

Lucifer turned toward him.

"Marcus, I swear to God, if you quote one more fucking Stoic—"

"There is no spoon."

"GET OUT."
