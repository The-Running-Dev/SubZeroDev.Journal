# I'm Not a Fucking Philosopher

*The Stoics, the Buddhists, the Zen masters, and one man who refuses to pay membership dues.*

Apparently, I have a philosophy problem.

I read philosophy.

I study Stoicism.

I practice ideas I've learned from Zen and Buddhism.

I find connections between these traditions, think about them, experiment with them, and apply what makes sense to my actual life.

And somehow, despite all this, I have absolutely no desire to call myself a philosopher.

Or a Buddhist.

Or a Zen master.

Or a Stoic emperor.

I just like learning shit.

Apparently, this is confusing.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Stoicism Department

I've spent years exploring Stoicism.

Marcus Aurelius. Epictetus. Seneca.

I've thought about what is within my control, what isn't, how I respond to events, and what it means to accept something without necessarily approving of it.

I've practiced these things.

Not perfectly.

Not every day.

And certainly not while wearing a toga.

But the ideas have influenced how I live.

At some point, Discount Store Marcus Aurelius appeared in my imagination.

"Excellent. You have embraced the Stoic path."

"Well, I've learned a lot from it."

"Then you are a Stoic."

"No."

He blinked.

"You study Stoicism."

"Yes."

"You practice Stoic principles."

"Yes."

"And yet you refuse the title?"

"Marcus, I'm not joining your fucking club."

He looked wounded.

"We have an excellent membership program."

"Of course you do. Do I get a discount at Lidl?"

"Only on selected philosophical products."

## And Then Came Zen

More recently, I began exploring Zen and Buddhism.

And something interesting happened.

I started noticing connections.

Not that they teach precisely the same thing. They don't.

They developed in different cultures, with different foundations and different understandings of human experience.

But I kept recognizing ideas that resonated with things I'd been practicing.

Acceptance.

Attachment.

Impermanence.

Our relationship with thoughts.

The difference between experiencing life and constantly trying to control it.

The Stoics talk about examining judgments and distinguishing what depends on us from what doesn't.

Zen places a different emphasis on direct experience, attention, and seeing beyond the concepts we continually manufacture.

Buddhist teachings explore suffering, attachment, impermanence, and the nature of self.

Different traditions.

Different frameworks.

But sometimes they seem to illuminate the same practical difficulty from different directions.

And I love that.

Not because I've discovered some secret formula that unifies Eastern and Western philosophy.

Because I'm finding ideas I can actually use.

Lucifer entered the imaginary discussion.

"Brother, are you telling me you've abandoned Rome for the Buddhists?"

"No."

"Converted?"

"No."

"Joined a monastery?"

"No."

"Purchased an orange robe?"

"No!"

"Then what the devil are you doing?"

"Learning, you magnificent asshole!"

Lucifer smiled.

"How inconvenient. You've robbed everyone of the opportunity to classify you."

## The Costco Philosophy Club

I can imagine the whole ridiculous enterprise.

A giant wholesale warehouse devoted to philosophy.

Welcome to Costco Philosophy.

Aisle one: Stoicism.

Aisle two: Zen.

Aisle three: Buddhism.

Aisle four: Existentialism, currently on clearance.

At the entrance, a helpful employee asks which philosophical identity I'd like to purchase.

"None, thanks. I'm just looking."

"Sir, you can't practice Stoicism without the Stoic membership card."

"Why the fuck not?"

"Company policy."

"And Zen?"

"Premium membership."

"Buddhism?"

"Comes with an eight-part starter package."

"Jesus Christ. Do you sell actual understanding?"

"Sorry, sir. Out of stock."

Lucifer would be laughing.

Discount Marcus would be arguing with the cashier.

And I'd be leaving with three books, absolutely no membership cards, and a receipt I couldn't fucking understand.

## Practice, Not Credentials

Here's what matters to me.

I'm not a Zen master.

I'm not an authority on Buddhism.

I'm not a professional philosopher.

I'm not a psychologist or psychiatrist.

I'm a man who encounters ideas, considers them, and sometimes finds something valuable enough to practice.

Sometimes a teaching makes sense intellectually.

Sometimes it doesn't.

Sometimes I revisit something years later and discover that my understanding has changed.

That's part of the experience.

I don't need to adopt an entire identity around something simply because it has influenced my thinking.

And I don't have to declare every philosophical tradition correct to appreciate what I can learn from it.

Discount Marcus looked at me thoughtfully.

"Perhaps the important thing is how one lives."

"That's what I've been saying."

"And not what one calls oneself."

"Precisely."

"Then perhaps you are more Stoic than you admit."

"Marcus."

"Yes?"

"Don't make me return you to the discount aisle."

## The Beautiful Absurdity

I've encountered people who speak with tremendous certainty about what humanity is, what people can become, and what never changes.

Meanwhile, the more I explore these ideas, the less interested I become in declaring that I've reached some final conclusion.

I can recognize that a practice has helped me without claiming it will help everyone.

I can experience something meaningful without pretending I've mastered the tradition that describes it.

And I can draw connections between philosophies without pretending their differences have disappeared.

Lucifer raised his glass.

"Brother, you've assembled a philosophical education from several civilizations and refuse to put anything on your business card."

"Exactly."

"No credentials?"

"No."

"No spiritual title?"

"No."

"No decorative certificate proclaiming enlightenment?"

"Absolutely fucking not."

He considered this.

"Then how will people know what you've achieved?"

"Maybe they don't need to."

For once, Discount Marcus didn't object.

I don't need to belong to every school from which I learn.

**I don't collect philosophies to tell people who I am. I practice things that help me live.**

And if the universe insists on assigning me a label, it can take a number and join the fucking queue.
