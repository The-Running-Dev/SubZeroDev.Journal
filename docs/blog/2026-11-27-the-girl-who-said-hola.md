# The Girl Who Said “Hola”
*The Lucifer Chronicles — An Unsolicited Greeting, a Hundred-Dollar Subscription, and an Interspecies Diplomatic Incident*

There are many things I find fascinating about humanity.

Your wars. Your religions. Your extraordinary ability to invent entire moral systems and then immediately exempt yourselves from them.

But occasionally, a single human being manages to encapsulate the absurdity of your entire species in one glorious word.

**Hola.**

Yes. Hola.

Allow me to explain.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Hundred-Dollar Kingdom

Some years ago, while living in Spain, I found myself wandering the peculiar digital corridors of a website called Seeking Arrangement.

For the uninitiated, this was a place where wealthy gentlemen and attractive young women could pursue what the establishment delicately referred to as *mutually beneficial relationships*.

A marvelous euphemism.

Men came with money. Women came looking for men with money. Everyone pretended this was terribly sophisticated.

And the privilege of participating in this grand marketplace cost me approximately **one hundred American dollars**.

The women, naturally, got in for free.

An interesting business model. One might almost suspect they knew which side of the transaction was funding the circus.

Now, I have no objection to mutually beneficial arrangements. Quite the contrary. I find honesty about one's intentions refreshing.

But here's the thing.

**Having something another person wants does not obligate you to worship them.**

Apparently, this rather elementary proposition had not been universally adopted.

## The Grand Entrance

One evening, a young woman from the Valencia area appeared in my inbox.

She sent me a photograph.

And beneath it, a single word.

> **Her:** Hola.

Oh, magnificent.

What poetry. What imagination. What a breathtaking display of conversational ambition.

Shakespeare himself would have thrown down his quill and retired in disgrace.

Now, I could have responded with another “Hola.”

We could have exchanged greetings for the next six months, gradually working our way toward the revolutionary discovery that we both possessed functioning keyboards.

But I was feeling charitable.

And perhaps a little mischievous.

> **Me:** That won't do. I'm sure you can do better than hi.

There.

An invitation.

A challenge, perhaps.

A chance to demonstrate that somewhere behind the photograph existed an interesting human being capable of producing more than one syllable.

What happened next was glorious.

## The International Incident

She laughed.

Then she asked whether I spoke Spanish.

Then she informed me that I could do better, too.

Fair enough.

So I explained.

Yes, I spoke Spanish. I had lived in Spain. My profile happened to be written in English, a fact she might have discovered through the ancient and mysterious practice of reading it.

And then I presented a simple question.

> Would you talk to a man who only contacted you with “hola” or “hi”? Make it interesting. Make it fun. It can't be that hard.

A fairly straightforward proposition, I thought.

I was not requesting a doctoral dissertation.

I was not demanding a sonnet.

I wasn't even insisting on proper punctuation.

Just a little personality.

Perhaps something interesting from my profile. A joke. A question. An observation about the weather. A declaration that she had once wrestled a crocodile and wished to discuss the experience.

Anything.

**But apparently, I had committed an offense against the established international order.**

She informed me that saying hello was normal.

That people must first establish whether communication was possible.

That men on this particular website sometimes couldn't respond because they hadn't paid.

In other words, I was being lectured on the proper etiquette of initiating conversations by a woman who had initiated our conversation with the linguistic equivalent of knocking on a door and staring silently at the person who answered.

Wonderful.

I offered a final opportunity for us to move beyond the diplomatic crisis.

> **Me:** Lol. Alright. I can explain it to you but it's not worth it. What can I do for you?

And that, dear reader, was when the wheels came off the carriage.

## The Court of Female Entitlement

Suddenly, we were no longer discussing conversation.

We were discussing *reality*.

She wanted to know how many women approached me on the street and immediately said something more interesting than hello.

She demanded to know how many women attempted to conquer me with witty opening remarks.

Then came the verdict.

> **Her:** Go back to reality.

Oh, darling.

You have absolutely no idea whom you're addressing.

But she wasn't finished.

Apparently, my request for a conversation containing more than one word had also demonstrated an intolerable degree of arrogance.

She questioned whether I spoke to people this way because I was on an app where women said hello to me.

And then she laughed at her own accusation.

At this point, the situation had become so delightfully ridiculous that I could scarcely contain myself.

Consider the circumstances.

I had paid a hundred dollars to enter a marketplace where women were seeking gentlemen willing to spend money on them.

She had approached me.

She had invested approximately four letters in the endeavor.

I had suggested that she might make the conversation interesting.

And now I stood accused of arrogance, unreasonable expectations, and apparent detachment from reality.

**All because I had declined to be sufficiently impressed by “Hola.”**

The irony was exquisite.

She was now composing entire paragraphs to explain why she shouldn't have to compose an interesting sentence.

All that effort!

All that passion!

All that righteous indignation!

Imagine if she'd invested a fraction of it in the opening message.

We might have had a lovely conversation.

Instead, we had a constitutional crisis.

## Enter the Prince of Darkness

Now, I have been called many things in my existence.

Arrogant.

Difficult.

Insufferable.

An asshole.

Occasionally, far worse.

It happens.

One grows accustomed to criticism after a few millennia.

But I find something deeply amusing about humans who approach you voluntarily, become offended when you don't behave according to their expectations, and then insist that *you* are the one detached from reality.

So I decided to stop explaining myself.

And start enjoying the performance.

I gave her the only response the occasion truly deserved.

> **Me:** Ha ha ha. Keep going. You are awfully entertaining. I don't live in reality… I am the Prince of Darkness after all. Obviously I've hit a nerve… you humans can be so touchy.

Ah.

There it was.

Perfection.

Not because it was particularly insulting.

Not because it resolved anything.

But because it was gloriously, unapologetically absurd.

She had instructed me to return to reality.

And I had responded by revealing that I was, in fact, **Lucifer Morningstar, Prince of Darkness, and a somewhat disappointed observer of the human species.**

The entire argument had ceased to matter.

I wasn't debating her anymore.

I was studying the wildlife.

And the wildlife was becoming increasingly agitated.

## A Brief Visit from Discount Marcus Aurelius

At this point, Discount Marcus Aurelius would undoubtedly emerge from the clearance aisle of Stoicism and offer some profound observation.

*“The obstacle to conversation is the conversation itself.”*

Thank you, Marcus.

Very helpful.

Perhaps something about controlling one's reactions?

*“You cannot control what others say, only how you respond.”*

Indeed.

And I responded by declaring myself the Prince of Darkness.

I consider that an entirely satisfactory application of Stoic philosophy.

Now return to your discount bin.

## The Beautiful Irony

Here's what I find most entertaining about the entire affair.

I wasn't pursuing this woman.

I hadn't approached her.

I hadn't begged for her attention.

I hadn't promised her money.

**She messaged me.**

And yet, somehow, I was expected to feel privileged merely because she had decided to make contact.

Lady, you knocked on my fucking door.

I answered.

You didn't like the reception.

And now I'm supposed to apologize because the accommodations weren't to your liking?

Please.

There's an entire world full of men who might be delighted to receive your “Hola.”

Go find one.

I'm sure you'll have a wonderful conversation.

Eventually, one of you might even introduce a second word.

## The Final Absurdity

Years later, I rediscovered the screenshots of this extraordinary encounter.

I'd been looking for them for ages.

And do you know what I realized?

**I couldn't remember what she looked like.**

Not really.

I'm sure she was beautiful. That was rather the point of the platform, after all.

But her face?

Gone.

Her name?

Irrelevant.

Whatever physical attraction might once have existed had completely vanished from memory.

Yet I remembered the argument.

I remembered the absurdity.

And, above all, I remembered my favorite line.

**“You humans can be so touchy.”**

That survived.

Not the beauty.

Not the photograph.

Not the possibility of an arrangement.

Just the memory of a woman who said “Hola,” became furious when I requested a little more imagination, and somehow ended up arguing with Satan about the nature of reality.

I paid a hundred dollars for access to that website.

And while I cannot say the subscription produced much of lasting value, it did provide one of the most gloriously ridiculous conversations of my dating life.

Some people spend their money on theater tickets.

Others go to comedy clubs.

I apparently subscribed to a service that delivered the entertainment directly to my inbox.

And occasionally, it was magnificent.

---

**A final word from Lucifer:**

My dear, I never asked you to kneel.

I never asked you to worship me.

I merely suggested that you might have something more interesting to say than “Hola.”

You could have ignored me.

You could have laughed.

You could have told me to fuck off.

Instead, you delivered an impassioned lecture on arrogance, reality, and the sacred protocols of approaching strangers.

And somehow, **I'm the one who doesn't live in reality.**

How delightfully human.

*Yours, eternally amused,*

**Lucifer Morningstar**

*Prince of Darkness*

*Former Paying Customer of Seeking Arrangement*

*Apparently Unqualified to Receive a Simple Greeting*

**P.S.** You approached me, darling. Nobody summoned you.

**P.P.S.** You humans really are so fucking touchy.
