# The Weekend That Made Absolutely No Fucking Sense

Lucifer appeared before God looking like he'd been dragged backward through a hedge, run over by a bicycle, and subsequently interrogated by the Bulgarian police.

God looked up.

"Good heavens. What happened to you?"

Lucifer pulled out a chair.

"Father, I would like to know the exact same fucking thing."

<!-- truncate -->

## It Started Like Any Other Weekend

There was no grand plan.

No elaborate expedition.

No carefully arranged appointment with disaster.

Just a weekend.

Some alcohol.

A few people.

An electric bicycle.

What could possibly go wrong?

God raised an eyebrow.

"Do you want the complete list?"

"Not particularly."

"Because it's rather extensive."

"Father."

"Very well."

Somewhere along the way, I lent my ENGWE electric bicycle to a man who was going to buy more alcohol.

An apparently reasonable decision within the extraordinarily questionable logic of that particular evening.

He returned.

The bicycle returned.

The rear tire, however, had apparently decided to retire from active service.

Completely fucking flat.

Lucifer stared at the bicycle.

"Wonderful."

## Enter the Bulgarian Police

At approximately eleven in the evening, the Bulgarian police stopped me.

The issue?

No helmet.

They checked my identification.

There was a conversation.

They eventually let me go with a warning.

In the grand catalogue of everything that happened that weekend, this was arguably one of the less ridiculous events.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius suddenly appeared beside the road.

"Remember, Lucifer, you cannot control external events."

Lucifer stared at him.

"Marcus, I'm standing next to a bicycle with a destroyed tire, being questioned by the police."

"Then control your response."

"My response is that I would like you to fuck off."

Marcus nodded.

"Excellent. A clear intention."

## Gravity Enters the Conversation

By approximately five in the morning, I was attempting to get home.

On the bicycle.

With the flat rear tire.

After drinking.

This was not one of humanity's great engineering decisions.

Somewhere along the journey, gravity decided to introduce itself.

More than once.

My face met the ground.

My hand took a beating.

My left knee joined the rebellion.

I remember falling at least twice.

Eventually, even I acknowledged the obvious.

The bicycle was not going to transport me home.

I was going to transport the bicycle home.

So I pushed the fucking thing.

God looked down.

"You know, I did invent walking."

"Yes, Father, and I eventually rediscovered it."

"After attempting to disprove gravity?"

"Are we going to do this all day?"

## The Missing Phone

Then there was the phone.

Somewhere during this magnificent sequence of decisions, I'd left it behind where we'd been drinking.

Because apparently having a flat bicycle, a bruised face, a painful hand, and a questionable knee wasn't quite enough.

The universe needed a missing electronic device.

Discount Marcus appeared again.

"Possessions are temporary."

Lucifer turned.

"Marcus."

"Attachment creates suffering."

"Marcus."

"One must learn to release—"

"IT'S A FUCKING PHONE."

Marcus paused.

"Then perhaps use Find My Device."

"That's the first useful thing you've said in your entire miserable philosophical career."

## The Bible Study

And then there was the Bible study I'd arranged with a friend.

Which I missed.

Not because I decided to reject spirituality.

Not because I'd undergone some dramatic philosophical transformation.

I was simply in no fucking condition to attend.

God folded his arms.

"So you missed Bible study."

"Yes."

"After being stopped by the police."

"Yes."

"After falling off your bicycle."

"Several times."

"And losing your phone."

"Correct."

God considered this.

"That's quite a weekend."

"Thank you for your penetrating analysis, Father."

## Monday: What the Fuck Just Happened?

Eventually, I slept.

And woke up.

And sat there.

Not with a great revelation.

Not with some magnificent insight.

Not with a newly discovered understanding of the universe.

Just sitting there, looking at the wreckage of an entirely unplanned weekend.

Wondering:

**What the actual fuck just happened?**

I wasn't trying to solve anything.

I wasn't searching for meaning.

I wasn't interested in some inspirational speech about adversity.

It was simply the sheer absurdity of events.

One thing happened.

Then another.

Then another.

And somehow, without planning any of it, I'd managed to assemble an entire weekend that sounded like a rejected screenplay for a particularly low-budget comedy.

God looked at Lucifer.

"Would you like to discuss your choices?"

"No."

"Reflect on your experiences?"

"No."

"Learn something from all this?"

"Absolutely fucking not."

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius stepped forward.

"Perhaps, then, the true lesson is acceptance."

Lucifer sighed.

"Marcus, there is no fucking lesson."

"But surely—"

"No. Sometimes things just happen. Sometimes they're ridiculous. Sometimes you wake up wondering how the hell you got from Saturday afternoon to Monday morning."

Marcus thought about this.

"And what should a Stoic do in such circumstances?"

Lucifer looked at him.

"Apparently, sit down and shut the fuck up."

## The Final Accounting

God produced a notebook.

"Shall we review?"

Lucifer groaned.

"Must we?"

"One flat bicycle tire."

"Yes."

"One police encounter."

"Yes."

"At least two falls."

"Yes."

"One missing phone."

"Yes."

"One missed Bible study."

"YES."

"One thoroughly bewildered Lucifer."

Lucifer stared at him.

"Father, is there a point to this?"

God closed the notebook.

"No. I just wanted to make sure I had the story straight."

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then God started laughing.

Lucifer looked at him.

And despite everything, he started laughing too.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius smiled knowingly.

"Amor fati."

Lucifer stopped laughing.

"Marcus?"

"Yes?"

"Go fuck yourself."

And somewhere in the distance, a bicycle tire quietly gave up on existence.
